Volume 5, May 2026

From Manuscript to Megabyte: Building Sustainable and Open Futures in the Digital Humanities

Edited by James B. Harr III, Emma Stanley, Jenna Rinalducci, and Donna Kain

The proceedings of the 2026 Digital Humanities Collaborative of North Carolina institute, From Manuscript to Megabyte: Building Sustainable and Open Futures in the Digital Humanities, organized in partnership with North Carolina State University Libraries, explore open, sustainable, and inclusive approaches to preserving and sharing knowledge across traditional and emerging media.

  • From Manuscript to Megabyte: Building Sustainable and Open Futures in the Digital Humanities
    James B. Harr III, Emma Stanley, Jenna Rinalducci, Donna Kain. 1–5.
  • Bringing Cultural Heritage to Campus through Innovative Technical Platforms and Physical Spaces
    Shaun Bennett, Franziska Bickel, Kevin Oliver, Angela Wiseman. 6–13.
  • The Manuscript as Dataset: Biological Evidence and Computational Analysis in Medieval Palimpsests
    James B. Harr III. 14–21.
  • Between the Lines: A Comparative Analysis of AI vs. Human Transcription of Lin Yutang’s Bilingual Handwritten Notebooks
    Guoying Yang. 22–37.
  • Reconstructing Historical Events with CIDOC CRM
    Yu Lee An. 38–47.
  • Google Map Reviews: A Window into Plantation Narratives
    Samone Jacobs. 48–57.
  • Opening Digital Humanities to All: Approaches to Supporting Accessible Omeka Projects
    Allison Kaefring, Jennifer Daugherty, Jeanne Hoover. 58–62.
  • From Working Group to Campus Resource: Strengthening Digital Humanities through the Library
    Christin Lampkowski, Savannah Lake, Jenna Rinalducci. 63–69.
  • Understanding Ephemerality and Loss in Digital Humanities Projects: What Surveys of the Field Can Reveal
    Kathryn C. Wymer. 70–76.
  • More Than Just Planting Pansies: Black Women’s Garden Clubs and the Neo-Archive in North Carolina’s Digital Landscape
    Rhonda D. Jones, Maurika Smutherman. 77–83.
